## Organizer reply (received before the talk)

Venue & equipment:
- Projector: yes, HDMI + USB-C (with adapter). Can be finicky but organizer is practiced.
- Wireless mic: yes, available on-site.
- Tables: yes, writable surfaces -- participants can write at them.

Audience:
- Size: 10-30 (varies month to month; at least ~10 since new space, up to ~30).
- Politically diverse across the spectrum. Smart people, hard questions.
- Strong feelings about AI in both directions -- morally opposed AND uncritical enthusiasts
  are both possible. Most attendees are nuanced.
- Small groups / pair work: encouraged, done regularly.

Format:
- Organizer does EFF-Austin intro (what EFF-Austin is, upcoming events, news), then
  introduces Benji's bio and hands off.
- Audience participation / interaction strongly encouraged.

Note: organizer described the talk as "educating people on how to use AI as a coding tool".
That was his own assumption; the submitted description (Overview/notes tab of the "LLM
Curriculum notes" Google Doc; event page https://effaustin.org/mental-models-for-working-with-ai-llms/)
has no coding framing. Its agent-forward opening ("Why are AI Agents so good at some things...",
"from simple chatbots to autonomous agents") may have suggested it.

## How it went

- The talk was a great success.
- Attendance: ~28 (28 take-home cards handed out); 21 entry-survey responses, 4 exit.
- Podium had a laptop stand and mic; laptop connected to the projector by a long cable,
  so neither a clicker nor the phone remote was needed.
- Projector resolution etc. was fine.
- Phone remote: the hotspot test worked at home beforehand, but on the venue WiFi the
  phone could not reach the laptop (device-to-device traffic appeared to be blocked).
- The laptop display went to sleep after a timeout (~10 min?); `caffeinate` did not
  prevent it. Matters most before/after the talk, when QR codes need to stay on screen.
